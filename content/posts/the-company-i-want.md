I've been thinking about this company for awhile now. I've told a lot of my
friends and colleagues. I was hoping at least one of them would start it for me
so I wouldn't have to but that doesn't seem to be happening. I'll try telling
all of you and then maybe it'll exist or I'll be forced into making it happen.

> This company exists to answer two questions that are extremely intertwined:
_how do I get experience if I can't get a job without experience?_ and _how do
you get new seniors if no one hires juniors?_ I don't think either of these
problems are new and neither is specific to tech. They pop up in, as far as I
know, all industries. Companies don't want to hire people without experience,
because training is hard and expensive. Then companies turn around and wonder
who they're going to hire to replace Alex because, oh boy, they're going to
leave soon and have a ton of knowledge. They also wonder why people aren't
sticking around longer than it takes to get a promotion or two. All of these
things are related.

I'm pretty sure a lot of this concept comes from my years in open source,
community conference organizing, and, of course, listening to punk music. And my
just general socialist/communist tendencies, I guess. A rising tide lifts all
boats, you know.

## Your first job but not your last

Here's the pitch:

> This company, a software consultancy, exists to give real-world experience to
newcomers so they can move on to a better job. By providing professional
software engineering projects through contracts with real companies, employees
will learn how their position works from beginning to end, whether that's in
sales, marketing, design, software, infrastructure, project management, product
management, engineering management, or somewhere else. By the time an employee
leaves, they'll know how to work in their career, they'll have made good
networking connections, they'll have demonstrable output to show, and they'll
have mentored others earlier in their position. The goal is to create a
well-rounded professional.

Yes, the employees are doing real work for real companies on real projects,
getting paid a real wage, getting real benefits while getting real experience.
Those that are interested will also be encouraged and supported in contributing
to open source projects. No, you can't start your startup on our dime, sorry.

My vision is usually a three-year tenure but three-to-five is probably more
realistic so I'll use five. During the first year of your job, you'll be trained
by an outgoing employee who has been doing that job for four years. Not the
hand-holding kind of training and not just sticking you in a corner with a book.
They'll act as a guide and mentor for the job, teaching you what they wish they
had known when they started the position. Each generation's training gets
better.

When you're in the last half of your fourth year, and all through your
fifth year, we go into prepardness, networking, and job placement overdrive. The
company wants you to have a new job with a different company. We want to help
you learn the field, make the connections, and then get out of here. You need
new experiences and challenges that we can't provide. We won't kick you out on
the street, of course! Your priority is to get even better at your job, our
priority is to show other companies how awesome you are.

Also during that last half of the fourth and all through the fifth year,
you'll be mentoring a new hire in your position. You aren't training your
replacement because you can't be replaced. You are, however, making your
department stronger by teaching this newcomer how to do the job as best they
can. Not only are you teaching them the technical and social aspects of the job,
you're also helping the team learn to work with this new person. No one likes to
feel new and alone and it's hard to incorporate a new team member, so we'll all
work together to make it easier.

The main point is that this is the job you get so you can get other jobs.
Outside of that, it also shows that a more compassionate approach to employment
is possible [^1] and that, if we all work together, we can improve things for
everyone [^2].

## Talent pool

When it comes to actually founding this company, I'm not sure what all the legal
and business ramifications are. I don't even know how to classify this; is it a
co-op? a B corp? a non-profit? All of the above? All I know is that we have a
problem getting people _into_ the workforce. They can't get hired because
they've only graduated from high school, or they don't have the right degree, or
they're fresh out of university. Maybe they're changing careers or coming back
after a long hiatus. Maybe they've recently left the military or have been
incarcerated before. Maybe they're unhoused. Whatever the situation is that's
out of their control, not being able to get into or back into their field
because of it doesn't help any of us. The job seeker doesn't have the ability to
pay for necessities, the companies don't have experienced hands to get the work
done, and the economy has less money moving around [^3].

The great thing about this approach, then, is that our talent pool is so much
larger than for other companies. We can find the gems that just need a little
cut and polish. We can refine the iron and make it stronger. We can cut away all
of the rock that isn't the sculpture. Will every single hire be this magical
untapped font of potential? No, of course not. But because we're chasing the
goal of producing an improved employee, not endless profits, we can take the
time and effort to find them. Just think of how amazing they'll be once those
five years are up!

And I'm not suggesting we'll hire everyone who sends us a resume. Again, no, of
course not. We'll have hiring standards just like any other company but they'll
be adapted to find the people we're looking for. Do I know exactly what that
looks like? Heck no. But if it's possible to hire like we do now, it's possible
to hire like this. Hiring is not something that only has a single successful
approach.

Once enough employees have ~~escaped~~ started new jobs, word will spread about
how great [^4] the hires from here are. That should make it easier to place
outgoing employees and bring in an ever wider net of possible hires. It might be
easier to start a staffing company, huh?

## Can it be done?

I don't know! I feel like I just said that. Maybe it's a crazy commie pipe dream
of mine but I don't see where it falls apart, if so. If you do, let me know!
I've watched so many engineers engage more and produce better and better code
because someone took the time to help and teach them. None of us make it through
life all on our own. If our career is going to be such a major part of that
life, it should be accessible, achievable, and it should feel like something we
can be proud of doing. I'd be very proud of this.
